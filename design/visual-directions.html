Step 01 — Visual direction

Notes you send, not notes you write.

Three takes on the same app. The layout contract is identical in all three — left rail, category page in the middle, chat composer on the right. What changes is how it feels to send yourself a thought. Pick one; we tune the palette after.

Language

Fixed in every direction

Left · category pane
Home calendar, every category with its colour dot and count, settings pinned to the bottom.
Middle · the selected page
Top quarter is the pinned urgent list. Below it, the thread split by day, newest at the bottom.
Right · the chat with yourself
Category chips (required), schedule and urgent toggles, then send. Same hand movement as Teams.
Replies are added notes
Long-press a note, add another. It lands underneath, quoting the original, on the opposite side.
01

DeskThe one that looks like work already

Direct descendant of Teams: near-square bubbles, tight leading, a single ink-indigo that never shouts. Opening this next to your actual work chat should feel like the same room — no learning curve, no new tool smell.

Type

IBM Plex Sans JP for everything — one family that draws Latin and Japanese from the same skeleton, so mixed lines never wobble. IBM Plex Mono for timestamps and counts.

What makes it this one

  • 7px bubbles with a 2px tail corner. Chat-shaped, but with the corner squared off on the sending side — the Teams tell.
  • Urgent notes are outlined, not filled. Same treatment in the pinned block and in the thread, so your eye learns one shape.
  • Category colour lives in the dot only. Bubbles stay one colour, so a busy day still reads as one calm column.
  • Dark rail, light canvas. The weight distribution every work chat app has trained you on.
02

TsuzuriPaper, threaded

Same mechanics, different material. Bubbles are outlined rather than filled, note text is set in Mincho, and timestamps sit in the margin like a ledger. It behaves like a chat and reads like a notebook — the right call if most of what you send yourself isn't work.

Type

Shippori Mincho B1 for note bodies only — Mincho is the signal that this is something you wrote, not something a system logged. BIZ UDPGothic for chrome, IBM Plex Mono for time.

What makes it this one

  • Bubbles aren't filled. A hairline and a 1px shadow only — a stack of slips, not a stack of chat messages.
  • Serif for your words, sans for the app's. The two voices never get confused.
  • 3px corners. Losing the roundness buys legibility when a note runs three lines.
  • Vermilion is reserved for urgent. Everything else is desaturated, so one red note carries the whole page.
03

YakanNight, phone-first

The LINE and WhatsApp lineage rather than the Teams one: large round bubbles, a dark ground, saturated category colours that survive being glanced at. Built for one thumb, in bed, at 23:40. Of the three this is the one that feels least like taking notes.

Type

M PLUS 1 throughout — even stroke weights that hold up as light text on a dark ground, where most Japanese faces smear. IBM Plex Mono for time.

What makes it this one

  • 15px radius, generous padding. Sized for a thumb before it's sized for a cursor.
  • Your bubbles are filled deep blue. The messaging convention you already obey: coloured means me.
  • Five saturated category hues. A dark ground is the only place you can run five strong colours without mud.
  • Doesn't blind you at midnight. If the notes you'd lose are the ones that arrive in bed, this is the one.

What happens after you pick

Choose one direction — or a hybrid, "01's structure with 03's night mode" is a perfectly good answer. The palette gets refined once you send me your colour instructions; these are starting positions, not final swatches.
